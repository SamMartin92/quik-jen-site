Dyslexia Assessment

For children and adults, at school, college or in the workplace.

A full diagnostic assessment looks at reading, writing, spelling and underlying cognitive processing skills using standardised assessment tools. It's suitable for anyone who suspects they, or their child, may have dyslexia, or who needs an up-to-date assessment for school, college or workplace access arrangements.

You'll receive a clear written report explaining the findings in plain language, along with practical recommendations for home, school or work.

Structured Literacy Instruction

One-to-one teaching built around how each learner learns best.

Structured literacy uses explicit, multisensory and cumulative teaching methods to build phonics, reading fluency, spelling and writing skills step by step. Sessions are planned around each learner's assessed strengths and needs, with regular review of progress.

This approach is widely recommended for learners with dyslexia, but is effective for any learner who needs extra support with literacy.

Handwriting Groups

Small groups building letter formation, fluency and confidence.

These groups focus on correct letter formation, pencil grip, spacing and writing fluency through short, structured, multisensory activities. A small group setting keeps sessions engaging while allowing individual feedback.

Ideal for children who find handwriting tiring, untidy or slow, and for anyone working on writing skills alongside a literacy programme.

Social Communication Groups

LEGO-based groups in a relaxed, structured setting.

These small groups use collaborative, LEGO-based activities to build turn-taking, listening, sharing ideas and working as part of a team. The structured, hands-on format makes social communication practice approachable and enjoyable.

Suitable for children who would benefit from extra support with social communication and interaction skills.
